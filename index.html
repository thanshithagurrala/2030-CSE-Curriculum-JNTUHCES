2030 CSE Curriculum – JNTUHCES

A student-led review of the R22 curriculum and a proposed curriculum for students graduating in 2030.

Explore Curriculum

My Biggest Finding

The R22 CSE curriculum has strong foundations, but several subjects need targeted updates so that students graduating in 2030 can connect those foundations with modern AI, software development, cloud, security and practical engineering work.

What I Found

KEEP

Strong Foundations

Data Structures and Algorithms remain important for problem solving and computational thinking.

UPDATE

Modernize Existing Subjects

Java, OS, DBMS, Networks, DevOps, ML, AI and Security need current tools, practices and practical work.

ADD / STRENGTHEN

Modern Skills

Deep Learning, Prompt Engineering, GenAI/LLM applications, Cyber Security, Cloud-Native development and AI/ML engineering.

2026 Reality and Industry

AI & GenAI

AI-assisted development, generative AI and AI agents are changing software work.

Cloud-Native

Cloud, containers, Kubernetes, CI/CD and observability are important production skills.

Security

Software supply-chain security and secure development need practical attention.

Developer Skills

Jobs commonly connect programming, databases, APIs, Git, cloud, testing, deployment and debugging.

What AI Could and Could Not Do

I tested AI using actual coursework from five subjects: CNS, ML, Compiler Design, Java and DBMS. AI was useful for explanations, calculations and starting code. Compilation, execution, testing, debugging, interpretation and adaptation still require student involvement.

Important: AI output was treated as a starting point, not as evidence.

What Could Prove Me Wrong?

  • Current graduate jobs may not require some proposed modern skills.
  • Employers may say the existing R22 curriculum is already sufficient.
  • The proposed curriculum may create too much workload.
  • R25 may already solve the main problems better.
  • Real AI testing may show AI-generated solutions are less useful than expected.
